Korean Used Car Export Process: Incheon Shipping, RoRo vs Container

After you buy a car in Korea, three things happen before it leaves: export deregistration, document preparation and booking space on a vessel. Shipping is either RoRo or container; here is how they differ.

The export steps in order

  1. Payment is confirmed and the car is reserved.
  2. The car is deregistered in Korea for export.
  3. Export documents are prepared: commercial invoice, export certificate and others your country needs.
  4. The car is delivered to the port and a shipping space is booked.
  5. The vessel sails and the bill of lading is issued.
  6. The documents are sent to you or your broker for import clearance.

RoRo shipping

On a RoRo (roll-on/roll-off) vessel the car is driven on and parked on a deck, like a floating car park. It is usually the lower-cost option for a single car, and it is common on routes with regular car-carrier sailings. The car travels in the open vessel space, and you generally should not leave personal belongings inside; ask your forwarder about the rules.

Container shipping

In container shipping the car is secured inside a 20 or 40 ft container, which gives more protection and lets you share a container between more than one car when sizes allow. It is often chosen for higher-value cars, for destinations without RoRo service, or when the buyer wants the car closed in for transit. Costs differ by destination and by how the container is filled, so ask for a quote for each option.

Which is better?

There is no single answer. RoRo tends to be simpler and cheaper for one car on a RoRo route; a container offers more protection and flexibility. The decision depends on your destination port, car value and the available sailings. We quote both when both are available.

Insurance

We recommend insuring the car for the sea voyage. Insurance can be arranged separately or included in a CIF quote.

After arrival

Your broker presents the bill of lading and the invoice to customs, pays duty and taxes under local rules and handles inspection and registration. Learn more in our step-by-step buying guide.

How to choose between RoRo and a container

RoRo (roll-on, roll-off) ships drive the car on and off, which is usually cheaper per car and faster to load, but it only works for cars that run and for ports that handle RoRo. A container protects the car from weather, allows two to four cars to share a container, and can carry spare parts or personal cargo as well. Containers are often chosen for landlocked routes, for destinations without RoRo service and for buyers who want to consolidate several vehicles.

Typical steps from payment to delivery

After the buyer confirms and pays, the car is deregistered for export, the export documents are prepared, and the booking is made with the shipping line. The car is delivered to the port, loaded, and the bill of lading is issued after sailing. The shipping line then sends an arrival notice at the destination, where the buyer or broker clears customs, pays the duty and collects the car. Transit time depends on the route and the transhipment points, so ask for the current estimate for your port.

Understanding FOB, freight and total landed cost

FOB (free on board) means the price covers the car up to the moment it is loaded onto the ship at the Korean port. Sea freight, marine insurance, port charges at the destination, customs duty, taxes, inspection, registration and inland transport are separate. To compare offers fairly, always ask whether a price is FOB, CFR or CIF, and then add the destination costs that your own broker quotes. A low FOB price does not help if the freight or duty makes the final cost higher than a slightly more expensive car in better condition. Documents you will normally receive

For a used car exported from Korea, the standard set is the export certificate (the proof the car has been deregistered for export in Korea), the commercial invoice, the packing list where applicable, and the bill of lading issued by the shipping line once the car is loaded. Some destinations also ask for a pre-shipment inspection certificate or a certificate of origin. Ask your customs broker which documents your country requires before you pay, and tell us early so that the paperwork is prepared in the right name and with the right consignee details.

Payment and risk: practical advice

Pay only against a written invoice that states the stock number, the full vehicle description, the price and the trade term, and pay only to an account in the name of the exporting company shown on that invoice. Be cautious of any seller who asks for payment to a personal account, offers a price far below the market, or refuses to provide extra photos. If you are unsure, ask us to send the invoice and the car details first and take time to check them. A genuine export deal has nothing to hide, and a reputable exporter is happy to answer questions before money moves.

Frequently asked questions

Do cars ship from Incheon only?

Incheon is the most common port for exports, and we typically ship from there. Other Korean ports may be used for some routes.

Can I put personal items in the car?

Rules differ between RoRo and container shipping and by destination. Ask us before loading anything.

Who arranges the shipping?

We can arrange sea freight on request and quote it separately, or you can use your own forwarder.

How long does shipping take?

Transit time depends on the destination and the sailing schedule. Ask for the next sailing to your port.
