Hyundai Staria Cargo Export from Korea: What Buyers Should Know

The Hyundai Staria Cargo is a van version of the Staria with a flat load area, popular with small businesses, delivery operators and tradespeople. Here is what to check before you import one from Korea.

What is the Staria Cargo?

The Staria Cargo is the commercial van version of Hyundai's Staria, with a rear load area in place of the rear passenger seats. It is offered as a 3-seater or 5-seater depending on configuration, with a diesel or a gasoline engine depending on the model year. Always confirm the seat count and engine for the specific car you are considering, because the listing spec is what you will receive.

What to check on a used Staria Cargo

Commercial vans may have been used heavily. Look at the mileage together with the year, the condition of the load floor and the rear door seals, and any signs of repainting or panel repair. Request photos of the cargo area and the underbody, and ask for the Korean vehicle inspection record. See how to check a Korean used car.

Import rules to check first

Some countries restrict imports by vehicle age, emission standard or steering side, and commercial vehicles are sometimes classified differently from passenger cars for duty and registration. Confirm with a local customs broker how a van with this seat count is classified in your country before you order.

Who buys the Staria Cargo, and why

Buyers are typically small logistics companies, bakeries and caterers, installers, rental operators and traders who want a modern van with a flat floor, a wide rear opening and a comfortable cabin. Compared with older vans, the Staria Cargo offers better driver assistance and a more car-like drive. Because the load area is shared with passenger-style comfort, it also suits owners who use one vehicle for both work and family. Check the payload and the load-area dimensions for the exact configuration, and confirm that they fit your regular cargo, such as pallets, boxes or tools.

Seats, engine and options to confirm

The same Staria name covers several body and seat layouts, so the stock number matters more than the model name. Confirm the number of seats, the engine type and the transmission, together with options such as the rear camera, parking sensors, cruise control and the infotainment screen. If your market uses a different fuel quality, ask whether the engine and emission system are suitable. Diesel quality and sulphur level can affect the particulate filter, so tell us the country and we can flag known concerns.

Understanding FOB, freight and total landed cost

FOB (free on board) means the price covers the car up to the moment it is loaded onto the ship at the Korean port. Sea freight, marine insurance, port charges at the destination, customs duty, taxes, inspection, registration and inland transport are separate. To compare offers fairly, always ask whether a price is FOB, CFR or CIF, and then add the destination costs that your own broker quotes. A low FOB price does not help if the freight or duty makes the final cost higher than a slightly more expensive car in better condition. Documents you will normally receive

For a used car exported from Korea, the standard set is the export certificate (the proof the car has been deregistered for export in Korea), the commercial invoice, the packing list where applicable, and the bill of lading issued by the shipping line once the car is loaded. Some destinations also ask for a pre-shipment inspection certificate or a certificate of origin. Ask your customs broker which documents your country requires before you pay, and tell us early so that the paperwork is prepared in the right name and with the right consignee details.

Payment and risk: practical advice

Pay only against a written invoice that states the stock number, the full vehicle description, the price and the trade term, and pay only to an account in the name of the exporting company shown on that invoice. Be cautious of any seller who asks for payment to a personal account, offers a price far below the market, or refuses to provide extra photos. If you are unsure, ask us to send the invoice and the car details first and take time to check them. A genuine export deal has nothing to hide, and a reputable exporter is happy to answer questions before money moves.
