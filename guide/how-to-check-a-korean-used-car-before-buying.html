How to Check a Korean Used Car Before Buying (Inspection Record Guide)

You cannot see the car in person, so verification has to come from documents and photos. Use this checklist before you pay for any used car from Korea.

Pre-shipment inspection examples

Examples of cars already exported. Not for sale; we can source similar cars.

1. Match the VIN and stock number

Ask for the VIN and check that it matches the number on the registration and inspection documents and on the photo of the VIN plate. A mismatch is a reason to stop.

2. Ask for the Korean inspection record

Used cars in Korea are commonly sold with a performance and condition inspection record that lists accident and repair history, the condition of main components and other findings. Ask the seller to provide it, and read the sections on frame and panel repair. If a seller cannot or will not provide any independent record, treat that as a warning sign.

3. Check mileage against age

Compare the mileage with the year of first registration. Very low mileage on an older car, or very high mileage on a young car, should be explained. Ask for the service history if available.

4. Review the photos

Ask for exterior photos from all angles, the interior, the dashboard with the ignition on, the engine bay, tires and the underbody. Look for panel gaps, paint mismatches, water marks and worn pedals or seats that disagree with the stated mileage.

5. Ask for a video

A short video of the cold start, the engine running and the dashboard warning lights tells you more than photos. Request one for any car you are seriously considering.

6. Check options and trim

Confirm the engine, drive type (2WD or 4WD), seat count and key options. Listings sometimes shorten the trim name, so ask for the full spec.

7. Put the agreement in writing

Confirm the car, price, trade terms and delivery in writing before payment, and keep the records. Continue to the full buying guide for the next steps.

A simple checklist before you pay

First, confirm that the listing shows the stock number, the exact model and grade, the year and month, the mileage and the colour. Second, ask for clear photos of all sides, the interior, the dashboard with the odometer, the engine bay and the underbody. Third, ask for the Korean inspection or performance record if one is available. Fourth, compare the mileage with the year; Korean cars are generally driven moderately, but a very low figure on an old car deserves a question. Fifth, ask whether the car has had accident repair or panel replacement, and whether the paint thickness was measured.

Warning signs worth noticing

Mismatched paint between panels, uneven gaps, overspray on rubber seals, new bolts on the front wings, water stains under the carpets and a musty smell can all point to repairs or flood damage. A price far below the market for the same car is also a warning. If something does not add up, ask for a short video walk-around or additional photos of the specific area, and do not pay until you are comfortable.

Why an independent inspection can help

If the car is expensive or you are new to buying from abroad, a third-party inspection before shipment can be worth the fee. It gives you a neutral report and photos of the points that matter. Ask us if you want to arrange it and tell us which items you want checked.

Payment and risk: practical advice

Pay only against a written invoice that states the stock number, the full vehicle description, the price and the trade term, and pay only to an account in the name of the exporting company shown on that invoice. Be cautious of any seller who asks for payment to a personal account, offers a price far below the market, or refuses to provide extra photos. If you are unsure, ask us to send the invoice and the car details first and take time to check them. A genuine export deal has nothing to hide, and a reputable exporter is happy to answer questions before money moves.

Frequently asked questions

What is a vehicle inspection record?

It is a document that records the condition and repair history findings of a vehicle. Ask the seller which record applies to the car you are considering.

Can you send an inspection record for a car on your site?

Message us the stock number and we will provide the available documents for that car.

Should I hire an independent inspector?

For high-value cars an independent inspection before shipment is a reasonable extra step. We can discuss this on request.

What if the car differs from the description?

Agree in writing before payment what happens in that case. Detailed photos and video reduce the risk.
