Kia Carnival and Hyundai Palisade Export from Korea: Buyer Guide

The Kia Carnival minivan and the Hyundai Palisade SUV are two of the most requested family vehicles from Korea. This guide compares them and shows current FOB examples.

Kia Carnival

The Carnival is a large minivan with sliding rear doors, offered in 7-, 9- and 11-seat layouts depending on market and year. Engines include diesel and gasoline versions. It suits large families, shuttle or tourism use. Check the seat count you need, because some countries register 9- and 11-seat vehicles in a different class.

  • Kia The New Carnival Diesel 2.2 9-Seater Gravity (2024, Gray): FOB US$29,300, 21,906 km.

Hyundai Palisade

The Palisade is a three-row SUV, offered in 7- and 8-seat layouts, with diesel and gasoline engines and 2WD or 4WD depending on the car. It is chosen by buyers who want SUV ground clearance and a premium cabin.

  • Hyundai Palisade Calligraphy 2.2 Diesel 2WD (2024, White): FOB US$31,700, 12,565 km.

Prices are FOB Korea as of 2026-11-04 and may change.

Carnival or Palisade?

Choose the Carnival for maximum interior space and easy access through sliding doors. Choose the Palisade if you prefer an SUV and may drive on rougher roads. Compare fuel type as well: diesel is common for long distances, gasoline for lower purchase price in some listings.

Why Carnival and Palisade are the most requested family vehicles

The Kia Carnival and the Hyundai Palisade are among the most requested Korean used vehicles for export because they offer seven to nine seats, a high level of equipment and strong demand on resale in many markets. Carnival buyers often use the car for family transport, hotel and airport shuttles or tour operators, while Palisade buyers usually want a larger SUV with a premium feel. Both are available with different engines and trims, so the grade name and the seat layout need to be checked on every listing.

Diesel, gasoline and drive type

Diesel versions are popular where fuel economy matters and where diesel is cheap, while gasoline versions are chosen where diesel quality is poor or where local rules favour gasoline. Palisade models may be 2WD or 4WD, which affects price and suitability for rough roads or snow. Ask which version you are being offered and make sure the invoice repeats it. Do not assume that two cars with the same model name and year have the same engine.

Seat layout and passenger rules

Some countries limit or tax vehicles by seat count, so a nine-seater and a seven-seater may be treated differently for registration and duty. Confirm the classification with a local broker before you choose, because it can change the real cost by more than the difference in the Korean price.

Understanding FOB, freight and total landed cost

FOB (free on board) means the price covers the car up to the moment it is loaded onto the ship at the Korean port. Sea freight, marine insurance, port charges at the destination, customs duty, taxes, inspection, registration and inland transport are separate. To compare offers fairly, always ask whether a price is FOB, CFR or CIF, and then add the destination costs that your own broker quotes. A low FOB price does not help if the freight or duty makes the final cost higher than a slightly more expensive car in better condition. Documents you will normally receive

For a used car exported from Korea, the standard set is the export certificate (the proof the car has been deregistered for export in Korea), the commercial invoice, the packing list where applicable, and the bill of lading issued by the shipping line once the car is loaded. Some destinations also ask for a pre-shipment inspection certificate or a certificate of origin. Ask your customs broker which documents your country requires before you pay, and tell us early so that the paperwork is prepared in the right name and with the right consignee details.

Payment and risk: practical advice

Pay only against a written invoice that states the stock number, the full vehicle description, the price and the trade term, and pay only to an account in the name of the exporting company shown on that invoice. Be cautious of any seller who asks for payment to a personal account, offers a price far below the market, or refuses to provide extra photos. If you are unsure, ask us to send the invoice and the car details first and take time to check them. A genuine export deal has nothing to hide, and a reputable exporter is happy to answer questions before money moves.
